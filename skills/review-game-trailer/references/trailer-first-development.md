# Trailer-first vertical-slice development

Use the trailer as a visual design test before full production.

## Build the beat sheet

Without writing public narration or title cards, plan a short sequence that can
show:

1. the core fantasy immediately;
2. the repeated player action;
3. a meaningful choice or system interaction;
4. escalating danger, complexity, or mastery;
5. progression or a change in capability;
6. environmental, enemy, character, or systemic variety; and
7. a satisfying payoff that leaves a further possibility open.

The exact count and order should fit the genre. The point is to expose what the
game must visibly prove.

## Convert beats into acceptance criteria

For each beat, record:

- the runnable scenario and player input;
- required mechanics, content, UI, art, animation, effects, and audio;
- what must be representative rather than temporary;
- capture conditions and reset procedure; and
- the failure that would make the beat unconvincing.

The vertical slice is ready when a competent player can produce the planned
footage in the real game. Staging a deterministic scenario is acceptable when
it preserves normal mechanics and presentation. Fabricating a visual result
that players cannot experience is not.

## Use the result to control scope

Prioritize work that strengthens the core loop, makes the genre legible,
creates meaningful progression, or supplies needed visual variety. Challenge
features that consume production time but do not improve the playable core or
any honest proof of it.

Trailer-first is not trailer-only. Test whether the resulting slice is fun and
understandable with outside players before scaling production.
