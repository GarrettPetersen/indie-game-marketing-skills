# Trailer shot and audio audit

## Opening and comprehension

- Show authentic gameplay and the core fantasy immediately.
- Do not spend an unknown game's opening on company logos, lore, black screens,
  or slow text.
- Make the primary genre and player action understandable without relying on
  narration.
- Treat the first trailer as a source for Steam's cropped and abbreviated
  presentations; verify current Steam behavior rather than assuming it.

## Footage integrity

- Use the newest build except where an explicitly labelled comparison requires
  an older build.
- Never stretch wide footage into a vertical frame. Capture natively or crop
  deliberately while preserving composition and legibility.
- Never alter the game's HUD to imply a normal gameplay state that does not
  exist.
- Show competent play: no grinding into terrain, purposeless movement,
  avoidable misses, wrong abilities, or confused menu navigation unless the
  point of the video requires it.
- Do not repeat the same clip without a clear editorial reason.
- Do not cut between supposedly continuous scenes with a different character,
  vehicle, location, weather state, loadout, or interface.
- Do not use obsolete UI or visuals accidentally.

## Readability and pacing

- Hold long enough for the viewer to understand an action and its result.
- Avoid both dead holds and frantic cuts that interrupt speech or game events.
- Either show a modal long enough to read or suppress it completely. Never
  flash it for a few frames.
- If text is intentionally shown, use exact human-written wording and give it
  sufficient reading time.
- Vary location, scale, direction, activity, and visual texture without losing
  continuity inside an explained sequence.

## Combat and event staging

- Begin at a distance and orientation that makes the intended tactic legible.
- Show attacks connecting when the trailer implies competence or power.
- Let consequential actions—harpooning, trading, rescuing, discovering,
  upgrading, or defeating—play through to a readable outcome.
- Suppress incidental achievements, tutorials, dialogue, or notifications that
  are not part of the beat.

## Audio

- Preserve natural attack, impact, and environmental tails across edits.
- Prevent cannons, impacts, UI clicks, and other transients from overpowering
  narration.
- Avoid abrupt music or ambience cutoffs; use motivated transitions and
  crossfades.
- Remove distracting clicks or mistakes without cutting into syllables or
  changing the speaker's meaning.
- Listen on speakers and headphones at ordinary volume.

## Quality assurance

- Inspect a review sheet across the whole cut.
- Inspect frames immediately before and after every cut, modal, caption, and
  overlay boundary.
- Confirm no source segment is duplicated unintentionally.
- Confirm every visible feature exists in the advertised build.
- Verify resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate, codecs, duration, channel layout,
  audio peaks, and platform file-size limits.
