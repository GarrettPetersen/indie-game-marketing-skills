# Performance benchmarks

Use benchmarks to answer a specific question at a specific lifecycle moment,
not to assign a permanent grade to the game. Retrieve the current
[How To Market A Game benchmark page](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/)
before reporting a tier because Steam surfaces and practitioner datasets
change.

## Apply the tiers correctly

How To Market A Game groups its benchmark observations around four gross
lifetime revenue tiers:

- Bronze: `$0–$10,000`;
- Silver: `$10,001–$249,999`;
- Gold: `$250,000–$999,999`; and
- Diamond: `$1,000,000+`.

The milestone values are correlations observed among games in those revenue
groups, not revenue promises or platform thresholds. A game can start slowly
and improve after a demo or another strong beat. Genre, page age, price,
region, release cohort, sample selection, and outside traffic all affect the
comparison.

Always:

1. identify the metric, measurement window, and project stage;
2. calculate the project's value from the best available raw export;
3. state the benchmark's study date and sample limitations;
4. report the closest tier or range without implying causation;
5. compare total and recent velocity when time affects the metric; and
6. end with the decision the comparison informs, not a vanity score.

## Current reference points

As retrieved on `2026-09-06`, HTMAG lists:

| Milestone | Bronze | Silver | Gold | Diamond | Study date |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Wishlists from coming-soon page launch | 100 | 500 | 1,200 | 7,000 | August 2022 |
| Typical weekly wishlists | 0–40 | 15–120 | 100–700 | 300–3,000 | August 2022 |
| Demo median playtime | 7 min | 18 min | 38 min | 65 min | 2022 surveys; 130 games |
| Wishlists earned during Next Fest | 0–999 | 1,000–6,999 | 7,000–9,999 | 10,000+ | February 2026 |
| Wishlists held at launch | 5,000 | 8,000 | 50,000 | 90,000 | Updated June 2026 |

Treat single values as tier reference points, not precise boundaries, unless
the underlying study explicitly defines them as ranges. The weekly ranges
overlap because they describe noisy groups rather than a deterministic ladder.
The coming-soon and weekly figures are older than the Next Fest and launch
figures; label them historical and look for a newer study before leaning on
them.

For February 2026 Next Fest, the underlying survey contained 182 responses and
roughly 170–174 usable observations depending on the field. Pre-festival total
wishlists were strongly correlated with wishlists earned during the festival,
but that does not prove that wishlist count itself caused the result.

## Wishlist diagnosis

Do not answer from the total alone. Report:

- net wishlists and gross additions/deletions if available;
- page age and lifetime wishlists per day;
- recent 7-, 14-, and 30-day velocity when the export supports it;
- gains attributable to each measured beat;
- current milestone, such as announcement, pre-demo, post-demo, pre-Next Fest,
  or launch runway; and
- comparison with both the relevant HTMAG tier and the project's own prior
  baseline.

Do not say the game has failed merely because its announcement was modest.
Conversely, do not let a strong lifetime total hide declining recent interest.
Explain whether the data calls for better presentation, a stronger demo beat,
more time, more relevant reach, or simply continuing the existing plan.

## Reporting format

Keep the answer compact:

| Metric and window | Project result | Benchmark | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |

Then give one to three actions. Clearly label HTMAG numbers as practitioner
benchmarks, not Valve requirements, algorithmic cutoffs, or guarantees.
