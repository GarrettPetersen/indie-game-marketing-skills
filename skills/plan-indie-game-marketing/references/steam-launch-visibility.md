# Steam launch visibility

Treat Steam visibility as a sequence to prepare for, not inventory the
developer can book. Keep Valve's published mechanics separate from practitioner
observations about unpublished thresholds.

## Manage the release date and Personal Calendar

Steam requires an exact intended date in the Steamworks backend even when the
public store page displays only a month, quarter, year, or `Coming Soon`.
Less-specific public dates are sorted at the last possible day of their range;
`Coming Soon` appears behind more specific dates in upcoming lists.

Use this project operating rule:

- announce and publicly display the exact release date at least one month in
  advance;
- prefer roughly eight weeks when the build is stable enough, matching the
  current Personal Calendar horizon and preserving a useful campaign runway;
- if the team cannot make that commitment confidently, stabilize and nearly
  finish the game first, then set the runway instead of repeatedly moving the
  date; and
- schedule a weekday release so the calendar shows the actual day and Valve
  staff are available if a launch problem requires help.

The one-month minimum and eight-week preference are campaign policy, not Valve
eligibility requirements. Valve permits less-specific player-facing dates.
Steam currently locks date changes two weeks before the specified date, when
additional upcoming-release visibility begins. Recheck the current rules before
committing because the Personal Calendar began as a Steam Labs experiment.

The Personal Calendar is individualized. Valve says it uses similar players'
wishlists and weights playtime profiles, retrains daily, includes already
wishlisted games, supports tag filtering, and looks across an eight-week
horizon. Do not promise that a wishlist count buys placement.

During the runway:

1. Verify that the first tags are specific, accurate descriptions of the core
   genre and play pattern. Do not churn them weekly to chase an opaque system.
2. Keep the capsule, opening trailer footage, screenshots, languages, demo, and
   store promise current and mutually consistent.
3. Schedule real beats across the runway: the date reveal, festivals or demo
   activity, creator coverage, human-written press and social pushes, and
   launch. Do not manufacture empty announcements.
4. Record Personal Calendar impressions, visits, click-through rate, wishlists,
   and daily wishlist velocity against a pre-runway baseline where reporting
   makes those fields available.
5. Change one testable store variable at a time and preserve the observation
   window. Never treat one game's movement as proof of the recommendation
   algorithm.

Use this default calendar, moving work earlier when a partner or festival has
its own deadline:

- `T-8 weeks`: freeze the launch window, publish the exact date as a full
  marketing beat, verify tags and store media, and capture the baseline;
- `T-8 to T-3 weeks`: space the strongest remaining demo, festival, press,
  creator, and social activity across the Calendar runway;
- `T-2 weeks`: treat the Steam date as locked, require the release build and
  packages to be approved, and stop making avoidable launch-risk changes;
- `T-1 week`: confirm every scheduled action, key, link, price, discount,
  localization, support shift, and rollback path; and
- `T-0`: release during staffed hours, execute the coordinated push, and begin
  the launch-health watch.

## Prepare for New & Trending

There is no New & Trending application or published threshold. Valve says new
games receive baseline launch visibility and may appear in New and Trending if
they are doing well; purchases and play are strong interest signals. Wishlists
mostly do not directly determine algorithmic visibility, but launch and
qualifying discount notifications can turn accumulated wishlists into paid
purchases.

Plan for New & Trending by concentrating genuine demand and successful play at
launch:

- do not shadow-drop; accumulate relevant wishlists, followers, mailing-list
  readers, press contacts, and creator relationships before release;
- synchronize the human-written launch announcement, release trailer, press
  outreach, creator coverage, website, community post, social posts, and
  wishlist notification around the release;
- make the build, purchasing path, install, controller support, save behavior,
  onboarding, and first session dependable so buyers can play rather than
  refund or wait for fixes;
- support the languages the game genuinely serves and use accurate tags so
  Steam can match it with the right players;
- set a price and optional launch discount that maximize the complete business
  outcome rather than unit conversion alone; and
- choose a supportable weekday and inspect the nearby release calendar, while
  refusing to trade away readiness merely to find an apparently quiet day.

Steam keys used for press and creators are access tools, not paid Steam
purchases. Their marketing value is the audience they may bring to the store.
Never buy, coordinate, reward, or misrepresent purchases, play, reviews, or
other activity to manipulate ranking.

On release day, assign owners for build health, customer support, community,
press and creator follow-up, and reporting. Watch sales, active players,
refunds, reviews, traffic sources, and the actual appearance of New & Trending
or other Steam surfaces. Keep a rollback or hotfix path ready. Record the time
of every material change so later analysis can distinguish cause from
coincidence.

Practitioner reports often describe New & Trending as being strongly affected
by launch revenue, but Valve does not publish a dollar threshold and the needed
performance can vary by market, timing, and competing releases. Use scenario
models, never a promised target.

## Ask Valve about a Steam client pop-up

Developers commonly call the prominent launch placement a Steam pop-up. Treat
it as a curated `Now Available` Steam client Marketing Message, not a paid ad or
a self-serve entitlement. Valve does not publish a guaranteed application
process or qualifying wishlist number. In its public documentation for full
release after Advance Access, Valve says it will contact games that are eligible
for a `Now Available` message.

Once the exact date is public and the launch has material evidence behind it,
ask an existing Valve contact, if the developer has one, whether the title can
be considered. Otherwise, the documented path to reach Valve is one concise
Steamworks Support ticket. This is a request for consideration, not an
application that overrides Valve's selection. Do it early enough for Valve to
evaluate the launch and request assets; do not wait until release day. Do not
pester support or send an unsupported request merely because a date exists.

Before asking, assemble a factual evidence packet:

- app ID, public store URL, exact release date, price, launch discount, and
  supported platforms and languages;
- current wishlist and follower totals, recent wishlist velocity, and dated
  sources for the figures;
- demo players, median playtime, repeat play, festival results, or other
  concrete product-interest evidence;
- verified press, creator, social, trailer, or community results;
- the coordinated launch schedule and any confirmed partners or coverage; and
- a current contact who can answer Valve and deliver correctly sized assets
  quickly.

The agent must not write the support request. It should ask the developer for
the exact wording, check the supplied text for factual accuracy and unsupported
claims, and preserve it verbatim. Opening or submitting the ticket is an
external communication and requires explicit authorization immediately before
submission.

Practitioner reports suggest games around 100,000 wishlists may sometimes be
considered, and that Valve may wait to observe real launch sales before making
a decision. This is anecdotal, not an official threshold, guarantee, or reason
to omit stronger evidence. Prepare to launch effectively without the pop-up and
do not claim it in the calendar until Valve confirms it.

If Valve expresses interest, respond quickly, verify all asset requirements
against the current instructions, and record the placement, market, duration,
and measured result. Do not assume a private conversation grants broader use of
Steam branding or confidential information.

## Output

Add a Steam launch-visibility section to the internal plan containing:

- backend date, public date, announcement date, lock date, release time, and
  timezone;
- the one-month minimum and preferred eight-week runway, with any exception
  explained;
- Personal Calendar baseline and weekly observations;
- the coordinated New & Trending launch runbook, owners, and health gates;
- the pop-up evidence packet, support-ticket status, and Valve response; and
- explicit labels for platform facts, practitioner evidence, and hypotheses.
