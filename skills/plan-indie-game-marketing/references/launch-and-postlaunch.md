# Launch and post-launch

## Release decision

Choose the date only after evaluating build stability, onboarding, demo and
playtest behavior, wishlist trajectory, creator and festival opportunities,
team capacity, platform review lead time, competing internal obligations, and
the sales required to sustain the project. Avoid treating one wishlist number
as a universal greenlight.

Check current Steam release, discount, seasonal sale, review, and visibility
rules directly in Steamworks. Use a safe internal content lock and leave room
for platform rejection, packaging, localization, controller, save, cloud,
achievement, and clean-machine testing.

Before setting the base price or launch discount, read
[pricing.md](pricing.md). Price is part of the launch positioning and revenue
plan, not a clerical field to fill in at the end.

Before fixing or revealing the release date, read
[steam-launch-visibility.md](steam-launch-visibility.md). Preserve enough runway
for the Personal Calendar and the release-date beat, and treat New & Trending
or client-message featuring as earned outcomes rather than promised placements.

## Coordinated launch

Prepare one truthful set of human-authored messages for the store, community,
press, creators, social channels, website, and mailing list. Reuse approved
language where appropriate. Do not create synthetic channel-specific voices.

Preserve a separate release-date announcement beat when the schedule permits.
Support it with a strong new trailer or gameplay asset, a human-written press
release and targeted pitch, an updated press kit, and a clear wishlist call to
action. Do not reveal the date casually and then attempt to recreate the news
later.

Verify before launch:

- final build and rollback procedure;
- price, packages, supported platforms, languages, and launch discount;
- current trailer, screenshots, capsules, press kit, and public links;
- creator keys and embargo details if used;
- support contact, known-issues policy, crash reporting, and response coverage;
- analytics or Steam reports required to judge the launch; and
- every scheduled external action and its owner.

Do not solicit reviews from key recipients as though they were purchasers, and
do not pressure testers, friends, or creators for positive reviews.

## After launch

Prioritize defects that prevent buying, launching, saving, controlling,
understanding, or completing the game. Distinguish current-build incidents from
old reports. Communicate fixes using human-provided public text.

Record sales, wishlist conversion, returns, reviews, traffic sources, creator
coverage, regional performance, support load, and play behavior. Compare each
beat to its intended result rather than celebrating impressions without a path
to store traffic or play.

Use meaningful updates and eligible discounts as later beats. Preserve the
lessons, reusable contacts, source files, and audience for the next game.
