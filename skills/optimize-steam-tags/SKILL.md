# Optimize Steam Tags

Treat the similar-games list as the primary diagnostic. Tags do not merely
describe the product: they help Steam decide which games it resembles and which
players may find it relevant. A coherent profile should place the game near
titles whose players genuinely share its buying expectations.

Accuracy is the hard gate. Never add a tag for prestige, reach, a festival, or
one minor mechanic when it would mislead a shopper about the repeated play.
Attracting the wrong comparison neighborhood wastes visibility and creates bad
expectations.

## Establish the intended neighborhood

Ask for or research:

- the current ordered tag profile;
- the current Tag Wizard similar-titles preview and public `More Like This`
  section;
- the game's actual core loop, structure, perspective, presentation, themes,
  and meaningful secondary systems;
- three to eight anchor games with substantially overlapping player appeal;
- adjacent games whose audiences may fit despite one important difference; and
- misleading lookalikes that share a theme or art style but serve a different
  player expectation.

Do not accept aspirational comparables merely because they are successful.
Explain what repeated activity, structure, mastery, or fantasy makes each
anchor's players a plausible audience for this game.

Read [references/similar-game-strategy.md](references/similar-game-strategy.md)
for the comparison matrix, ordering method, diagnostics, and maintenance
workflow.

## Build an accurate profile

Use the current Steam Tag Wizard and current official tag catalog. Consider up
to 20 accurate tags because Steam uses the top 20 for tag pages, similarity,
and tag-influenced recommendations. Make the top five a compact, recognizable
picture of the game, and order the first 15 carefully because some filters
prioritize them.

Prefer the most specific true subgenre, structure, mechanics, and player
activities that distinguish the intended audience. Broad or redundant tags
such as `Indie`, `Action`, or `Singleplayer` usually belong below informative
tags. A visual, theme, or mood tag may rank highly when it materially defines
the audience rather than merely decorating the game.

Judge the combination, not isolated tags. Valve says recommendation similarity
is strengthened by overlap among multiple top-20 tags, and that less-common
relevant tags can carry more information than extremely common ones.

## Validate against similar games

Run the proposed profile through the Tag Wizard and inspect the resulting
similar-titles list before publishing. The list should contain anchor games or
credible substitutes for understandable reasons. Diagnose every conspicuous
mismatch; do not force a desired title by adding an inaccurate tag.

Keep the distinction clear:

- the Tag Wizard preview is a strictly tag-driven similarity diagnostic;
- the public `More Like This` section uses the top 20 tags but is not promised
  to match the preview exactly; and
- personalized Steam recommendations use several systems, some of which use
  tag overlap with games a customer has played.

After an authorized publish, record the previous profile, new profile, date,
preview, public similar-game results, and Steam traffic observations. Community
tagging can change weights over time, so revisit drift without blindly churning
the profile.

## Output

Return:

- the intended player neighborhood and evidence for every anchor game;
- a current-versus-proposed ordered top-20 tag table;
- the role and accuracy evidence for each proposed tag;
- expected additions and removals from the similar-games neighborhood;
- rejected tags and the expectation they would misrepresent;
- screenshots or records of the Tag Wizard preview and public `More Like This`
  section when accessible; and
- the next measurement date and condition that would justify another change.

Recommend predefined Steam tags freely; selecting taxonomy labels is not
original marketing-copy authorship. Do not publish tag changes without explicit
authorization immediately before the external action. Publishing tags is
separate from publishing other store-page changes.
